# Decode Wisdom: Deconstructing Human Intelligence — 1+1=1+1, How Much Do You Know?

> What if the equation `1 + 1 = 1 + 1` holds the key to the limits of human intelligence?

## Background

In 1985, Charles H. Bennett and Rolf Landauer published a landmark paper — *"The Fundamental Physical Limits of Computation"* (Scientific American, Vol. 253, No. 1, July 1985, pp. 48–57) — showing that information is never truly destroyed in reversible computation. The expression `2 + 2` contains more information than `= 4`, because from the result alone, you cannot recover the original inputs.

This project builds on that foundation to explore a deeper question:

**What are the limits of human intelligence?**

## Core Idea

The scientific spirit — the disciplined pursuit of truth through observation, hypothesis, and verification — can serve as a unifying framework that integrates:

- **Science** — the laws governing information and computation
- **Philosophy** — the nature of mind, knowledge, and understanding
- **Knowledge** — the accumulated record of human discovery
- **Wisdom** — the capacity to act on what we know

At the center of this framework is a simple but radical idea: **`1 + 1 = 1 + 1`**

Not `1 + 1 = 2`. The equation preserves the full information of both sides — nothing is lost, nothing is reduced. If intelligence is the processing of information, then the limit of intelligence is not about how much we can compute, but **how much information we can preserve without loss.**

This directly extends Bennett and Landauer's insight: that reversible, information-preserving computation has no fundamental physical lower bound on energy cost. Perhaps the same principle applies to intelligence itself — that the ceiling of human understanding is not a wall, but a horizon that recedes as we learn to preserve more of what we know.

## Why This Project Exists

This project exists for three reasons:

**1. Personal Research**

The author is an independent researcher based in Hong Kong, starting to learn computing in 1982, from Apple II Plus 6502 assembly and BASIC programming, over 40 years.

**2. A Critique of the World**

Too many people think using the wrong methods. This is not an accusation, but an observation. Existing education and knowledge systems teach people how to memorize answers, but do not teach them to understand the structure and limits of thinking itself. The result: people believe they are thinking when they are merely repeating. This project asks a question most people cannot yet formulate: **not whether we can think, but whether we can think without losing what we know.**

**3. Changing the World to Make It More Reasonable**

The author wants to live in a reasonable place. And reasonable places do not appear on their own — someone must change them. The ultimate goal of this project is not to publish a paper, but to promote a more precise way of thinking that brings the world closer to reason. The intellectual environment in Hong Kong is dominated by exam-oriented education (see [Teaching to the Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaching_to_the_test)), which prioritizes standardized answers over original thought. New theoretical frameworks do not emerge from systems that penalize divergence — so the breakthrough must come from outside.
