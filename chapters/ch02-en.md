# Chapter 2: Mathematical Concepts

*The limits of mathematics in real life.*

## 2.1 Is 27.0000001 cm Equal to 27 cm?

In mathematics, 27.0000001 is not equal to 27. This is clear and true — the two numbers are different by 0.0000001, and mathematics does not allow any grey area.

But in real life, the answer depends on how you use it.

When you build a house, 27.0000001 cm and 27 cm make no difference. No worker will redo a job because of a 0.0000001 cm difference. In this case, the two numbers are equal.

But when you make a 2nm CPU, 0.0000001 cm is 1 nm — a full generation apart. In this case, the two numbers are very different.

The same numbers, in the same real world, are sometimes equal and sometimes not — it depends on how you use them.

The answers from mathematics do not directly apply to real life. Real life can of course learn from mathematics, but what matters more is how to use it.

This brings up a question worth thinking about: mathematics can be very perfect, but the perfection of mathematics is exactly how far it is from real life. The perfection of real life is never the same as the perfection of mathematics — the perfection of real life always depends on the situation, and each case can change.

So why is the perfection of real life different from the perfection of mathematics? The next part explains — with an apple.
